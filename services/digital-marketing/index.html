Digital Marketing

Connect a specific offer with a defined audience, a useful landing page, and a plan for learning what works.

In short: agree the campaign goal, align the message and destination, set approval boundaries, and choose evidence worth reviewing.

When this service fits

Start here when an agreed offer needs a focused campaign rather than activity across every available channel.

This outline suits a planned launch or an existing campaign with unclear priorities. Establish that the offer is understandable and the destination page is usable before expanding promotion. If the site cannot explain the service or support the intended action, address that foundation first instead of sending more visitors into a confusing experience.

Scope and deliverables

Turn a broad marketing request into a bounded brief with clear messages, responsibilities, and review questions.

  • A campaign goal, audience definition, offer, and intended visitor action.
  • A channel recommendation based on available assets and operating constraints.
  • A message and creative outline aligned with the landing page.
  • A budget and approval framework distinguishing service fees from media spend.
  • A measurement plan defining signals, reporting questions, and an initial review point.

Advertising spend, platform fees, creative production, account setup, and ongoing management need separate agreement. This website does not activate campaigns, purchase ads, or install tracking.

Process and review criteria

Check the entire journey from message to destination before judging a campaign by surface-level activity.

  1. Clarify: define the goal, audience, assets, and operating limits.
  2. Align: choose a manageable channel mix and check landing-page relevance.
  3. Approve: confirm messages, spend limits, access, and any measurement implementation.
  4. Learn: review evidence, explain uncertainty, and prioritise the next experiment.

Useful review criteria include message consistency, working destination links, approved spend boundaries, and meaningful outcome definitions. Impressions and clicks alone do not establish business value. Results depend on factors outside the service and cannot be guaranteed.

Prepare your brief

Bring a clear offer, a destination page, and someone authorised to approve both messages and costs.

Share the audience, existing assets, timing constraints, approved budget range, and previous campaign learnings. Explain what a meaningful outcome looks like. Any analytics or advertising integration requires a separate review of access, consent, applicable rules, and data handling before implementation.

Common questions

Set expectations about campaign readiness and what approval does, and does not, authorise.

Do we need to use every marketing channel?

No. Start with channels suited to the audience, offer, resources, and review capacity. A small, coherent campaign is easier to evaluate than disconnected activity that cannot be maintained or measured usefully.

Does a campaign brief authorise advertising spend?

No. Actual spend, account changes, publishing, and tracking implementation require the appropriate explicit approvals. A planning document is not an instruction to charge an advertising account or collect visitor data.
