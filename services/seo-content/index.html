When this service fits

Start here when pages are difficult to discover, overlap in purpose, or leave important visitor questions unanswered.

Useful search content explains the service instead of repeating keywords. This outline suits sites with generic descriptions, unclear headings, disconnected service pages, or a growing list of content ideas without clear priorities. Review the audience's real questions and existing evidence before deciding that a new page is necessary.

Scope and deliverables

Give each priority page a distinct job and enough verified information to help a reader make a decision.

  • An inventory of priority URLs, intended audiences, and page purposes.
  • Search-intent and question mapping based on agreed research sources.
  • Recommendations for unique titles, descriptions, headings, and section summaries.
  • Content briefs or revisions explaining fit, deliverables, boundaries, and next steps.
  • An internal-link map with descriptive labels and a reason for each relationship.

Technical implementation beyond agreed edits, original research production, and translation are separate scope items. Rankings, traffic, and lead volumes are not guaranteed.

Process and review criteria

Prioritise information gaps, verify claims with a subject expert, and record what changed.

  1. Review: inspect current pages, navigation, recurring questions, and available search evidence.
  2. Map: assign a distinct purpose and relevant next step to each page.
  3. Write: create useful explanations and have the owner verify service facts.
  4. Publish: check the rendered copy, metadata, links, and review schedule.

Review quality by whether readers can understand the offer, its limits, and the next action. A short page is not automatically weak, and a long page is not automatically useful. Word counts are review signals, not search-engine requirements.

Prepare your brief

Bring accurate service knowledge and evidence of what visitors need to know.

Share the page list, approved service descriptions, common customer questions, and any search reports you can lawfully provide. Name a subject expert who can validate claims. Identify outdated material and explain which audience or business priority should guide trade-offs.

Common questions

Separate editorial quality from ranking promises and distinguish updating a page from creating another overlapping page.

Is there a minimum word count for a service page?

No universal minimum establishes quality. The page should answer its audience's decision questions without padding. An editorial review can use a length threshold to trigger inspection, but factual completeness and clarity matter more.

Should every new question become a separate page?

Not necessarily. Improve an existing page when the question belongs to the same visitor need. Create a separate page only when it has a distinct purpose, enough useful material, and a place in navigation.
