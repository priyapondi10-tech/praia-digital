Web Design & Development

When this service fits

Start here when the website's structure or implementation prevents people from finding and using important information.

This outline suits a new service website or a focused rebuild. Common starting problems include inconsistent layouts, unclear navigation, and pages that are difficult to update. A small copy correction may not need a rebuild; first establish whether the problem is content, design, or the underlying implementation.

Scope and deliverables

Define a concrete set of pages and reusable patterns rather than an open-ended promise to redesign everything.

  • A page inventory, navigation map, and agreed purpose for each priority page.
  • Responsive layouts with meaningful headings, descriptive links, and keyboard-accessible controls.
  • Implementation of approved content and a consistent shared style system.
  • A launch checklist covering links, metadata, critical journeys, and known limitations.
  • Editing instructions and a handover identifying the source, hosting, and approval owners.

Domain changes, content migration, third-party integrations, custom applications, and backend forms require explicit agreement. A static page does not send enquiries merely because it displays a form.

Process and review criteria

Review the structure before the build and test the agreed visitor journeys before publishing.

  1. Discover: review the audience, existing assets, constraints, and most important actions.
  2. Plan: approve the page map, content responsibilities, and design direction.
  3. Build: implement the agreed layouts and review them on narrow and wide screens.
  4. Release: check links and content, verify deployment, and document remaining issues.

Useful acceptance criteria include reachable service pages, working keyboard paths, readable content at zoom, correct page titles, and a documented editing route. Formal accessibility certification and performance guarantees are not implied.

Prepare your brief

Bring the material needed to make decisions and identify who has authority to approve the result.

Gather the current website address, brand assets, draft copy, required pages, and examples of visitor problems. Name a content approver and a technical owner. Explain any migration, hosting, language, or integration constraints before the scope is agreed.

Common questions

Resolve content and maintenance responsibilities early so the launch does not create an ownership gap.

Should content be planned before design?

Yes. Page purpose and draft content help determine the structure. The content does not need to be final on day one, but ownership and approval dates should be agreed before layouts depend on it.

What happens after the website launches?

The handover should identify who edits pages, approves releases, and checks the live result. Ongoing maintenance is a separate responsibility, not an automatic consequence of completing the build.

Related services and next steps

Connect planning, promotion, and upkeep to the build only where they solve a specific project need.

  • SEO & Content clarifies search intent, page explanations, and internal links before or alongside design.
  • Digital Marketing aligns campaign messages with suitable landing pages once the destination is ready.
  • Website Care defines the recurring checks and change process after launch.
