Website Care

Keep a live website accurate and maintainable through routine checks, approved updates, and a documented change process.

In short: assign ownership, check important pages, make agreed changes, verify the public result, and escalate larger work into a separate scope.

When this service fits

Start here when a live website needs a repeatable upkeep process rather than an unplanned series of small fixes.

Service facts change, links move, and release checks are easily missed when nobody owns them. This outline suits an existing site that needs regular attention matched to its platform and risk. A substantial redesign, content strategy, or incident response should not be hidden inside a routine maintenance description.

Scope and deliverables

Agree the review frequency, access boundaries, and types of changes before defining a maintenance routine.

  • An inventory of hosting, source files, critical pages, and approval owners.
  • A recurring checklist for navigation, service facts, links, and deployment status.
  • Approved content corrections and explicitly scoped small technical changes.
  • A release record identifying what changed and how the live result was checked.
  • A prioritised backlog for structural work, larger rewrites, or unresolved risks.

Emergency response, round-the-clock monitoring, malware removal, hosting charges, and guaranteed uptime are excluded unless separately agreed. Backups, restore procedures, and platform updates depend on the actual technology; they must be documented, not assumed.

Process and review criteria

Make changes traceable and verify the deployed website, not just the source file.

  1. Inventory: identify the platform, access owners, critical journeys, and known issues.
  2. Agree: record review frequency, approval rules, and escalation boundaries.
  3. Maintain: make approved changes through a documented release process.
  4. Verify: check public pages and links, record the result, and flag work needing a new decision.

A useful care record names the revision, checks performed, issues found, and responsible next owner. For this static site, Git history can recover source changes but does not back up unrelated databases or external services.

Prepare your brief

Bring platform facts and ownership information so the care plan matches the real site.

Provide the website address, hosting and repository details, approval contacts, recurring issues, and business-critical pages. Agree a secure process for granting the minimum necessary access. Do not place passwords, tokens, customer data, or private incident details in a public repository.

Common questions

Clarify the difference between routine upkeep, incident response, and larger improvement projects.

Does website care include emergency support?

Not by default. Response times, coverage hours, escalation contacts, and incident responsibilities require an explicit agreement. A recurring checklist is not the same as an emergency support commitment.

When does a change become a separate project?

Changes that alter the site structure, introduce integrations, or require substantial new content should be assessed separately. Record the need in the backlog and agree scope, risk, cost, and review criteria before starting.
